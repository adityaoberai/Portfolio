# oberai.dev — Personal Website Specification (Version 1)

## Vision

Build a static personal website that serves as the definitive home for Aditya Oberai on the internet.

The goal is not to build a developer portfolio or migrate existing content platforms. Instead, the website should present a thoughtful overview of Aditya's work, interests, and accomplishments while directing visitors to the platforms where that work already lives.

The website should feel like meeting the person behind the work—not reading a resume.

---

# Goals

The website should help visitors quickly understand:

- Who Aditya is
- What he works on
- Why his work matters
- How to collaborate with him
- Where to find his writing, photography, projects, and talks

It should support opportunities such as:

- Developer Relations leadership
- Conference speaking
- Community collaborations
- Technical consulting
- Hiring conversations
- Personal brand growth

---

# Core Principles

The website is:

- Static
- Fast
- Easy to maintain
- Editorial
- Personal
- Professional
- Timeless

The website is **not**:

- A CMS
- A blog
- A newsletter platform
- A photography gallery
- A GitHub portfolio clone
- A resume website

# Design Direction

## Overall Feel

The website should feel like a thoughtful personal home rather than a polished corporate portfolio.

Target characteristics:

- Editorial
- Minimal
- Warm
- Calm
- Spacious
- Confident
- Human

Visitors should feel as though they're exploring a carefully curated publication rather than browsing a landing page.

---

## Visual References

Draw inspiration from:

- Stripe Press
- Linear
- Read.cv
- Monocle
- The Browser Company
- Vercel editorial pages

These references share:

- Excellent typography
- Generous whitespace
- Strong hierarchy
- Restrained use of color
- Elegant layouts

---

## Layout Principles

Use a spacious, typography-led layout.

Guidelines:

- Wide but readable content columns
- Consistent vertical rhythm
- Large section spacing
- Editorial layouts over dense grids
- Cards only where they improve scanning
- Strong mobile-first hierarchy

---

## Typography

Typography should define the personality of the site.

Suggested approach:

- Elegant serif or humanist display font for headings
- Clean sans-serif for body copy
- Monospace only for metadata, tags, or technical snippets

The overall experience should resemble a well-designed magazine profile more than a resume.

---

## Color Palette

Keep the palette restrained.

Suggested foundation:

- Soft off-white background
- Charcoal text
- Warm gray borders
- One muted accent color
- Optional subtle secondary accent for hover states

Avoid loud gradients, neon colors, and visually noisy interfaces.

---

## Imagery

Photography should be used intentionally, not extensively.

Ideas:

- A single strong portrait or candid image on the homepage or About page
- Small visuals alongside community initiatives
- Occasional project screenshots
- Link to Pexels for the full photography archive

---

## Motion

Motion should support the experience without drawing attention to itself.

Appropriate interactions:

- Gentle hover states
- Subtle transitions
- Small page fades
- Smooth scrolling

Avoid:

- Scroll-jacking
- Complex page transitions
- Heavy animation
- Decorative effects that slow the site

---

## Content Style

Writing throughout the site should be:

- Human
- Specific
- Reflective
- Confident
- Free of unnecessary jargon

The emphasis should always be on clarity and authenticity.

---

## Desired Visitor Experience

A visitor should leave thinking:

> "Aditya works on interesting problems, communicates them clearly, and builds meaningful communities."

Not simply:

> "This is a nice portfolio."

This website should become the canonical home for Aditya Oberai online.
